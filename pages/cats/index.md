I am the primary caretaker for a small clowder:

* Ginger is a battle-scarred, asthmatic, pre-diabetic rescue tabby. We got her
  when she was maybe two years old in 2013. She's the grizzled, grouchy alpha,
  and is friendly enough to the others so long as they respect her primacy.

* Pickles is a nervous black cat, prone to puking and cowering in fear. She is
  also a rescue, painstakingly won over as a kitten by my wife in October of
  2020.

* Puffin is an absurdly-friendly black-and-white kitty, rather clumsy, and an
  inveterate optimist. She's a rescue in some sense, but she rescued herself
  - she walked into our lives and home quite literally on March 3, 2022, when
  she scratched at our front door, rather battered and skinny as a rail. She
  has the distinction of being a living refutation of utilitarianism, as she is
  an in-the-flesh utility monster (she enjoys life far more than most
  creatures, deriving more utils from a minute of head-scratches than some
  beings seem to get from a three-week luxury cruise).

## Care Routines

### Feeding

All three cats get fed breakfast and dinner. Breakfast is usually around 8:30
AM, then have dinner around 6 PM.

Cat food is under the kitchen sink, on the left-hand side, as are Ginger and
Pickle's bowls. Ginger's is yellow and Pickles' is blue.

Puffin's larger blue bowl is on the hutch in the dining room, where she grazes
from it throughout the day.

At both mealtimes, I take all three bowls and the two bags of food to the
island. Puffin and Pickles each get one scoop of the sensitive-stomach cat
food, using the smaller scoop in the clear pastic container of kibble. I then
take their food out to the dining room and put Puffin's on the left side of the
doorway, and Pickle's food to the right side of the doorway.

I then serve Ginger's food and medications, which differs between the morning
and evening.

For breakfast, Ginger gets a half-pill of prednisolone wrapped in a half-pill
pocket, followed by one small scoop of prescription kibble. The prednisolone
and pill pockets are in the cabinet above the refrigerator, while the
prescription food is in a red-and-white bag under the sink.

For dinner, Ginger gets a theophylline pill crushed and mixed with a spoonful
of wet food, which is then mixed with a half-scoop of her prescription kibble.
I crush the theophylline by using one of our heavy tablespoons and a cereal
bowl as a mortar and pestle, then use the spoon to get a scoop of wet food and
mix it with the theophylline. I then put a quarter-scoop of kibble in the
bottom of her regular bowl, put the wet food mixture on top, and stir until
combined. I sprinkle the remaining dry kibble on top, then give her the bowl.

While the cats eat, I stay and watch them, as they're prone to steal each
other's food. As soon as one leaves their bowl, I pick it up and put it away.


### Water

We refill a small bowl of water daily. It's in the master bathroom, next to the
tub.

There's also a large water bowl in the garage, which I rinse out and refill
daily. It can last at least two days without a refill if needed.


### Litterboxes

We scoop litterboxes daily. Pet waste baggies are under the kitchen sink.

There's one indoor litterbox in the library, which is rarely used but should be
checked daily. It is Pickles' security box, when she's feeling stressed or
afraid of the garage.

There are five more litterboxes in the garage. I keep them all filled, and they
are used in an unpredictable rotation by all of the cats.

We use [Dr. Elsey's CatAttract
litter](https://www.drelseys.com/products/cat-attract-litter/) in Pickles'
security box and several of the garage boxes. In the flat box, we combine it
with [Ökocat litter](https://okocat.us.com/index.html). I would swap over to
Ökocat entirely, but Pickles does not seem to like it.
